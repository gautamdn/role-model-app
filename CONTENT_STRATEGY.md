# Content Strategy

## Overview

Content is the heart of the Role Model App. This document outlines our approach to curating, creating, and maintaining high-quality, age-appropriate content that teaches character development without promoting unhealthy celebrity worship.

---

## Content Philosophy

### Core Principles

1. **Process Over Outcome**
   - Focus on how role models developed traits, not their achievements
   - Emphasize effort, practice, and growth
   - Include failure stories and lessons learned

2. **Multi-Source Inspiration**
   - Show 3-5 role models per trait
   - Prevent fixation on any single person
   - Demonstrate that traits can be developed in many ways

3. **Age-Appropriate Content**
   - Simple language and concepts for kids (5-10)
   - More nuanced stories for tweens/teens (11-17)
   - Different examples and activities per age group

4. **Universal Applicability**
   - Every child can practice these traits
   - No special circumstances required
   - Emphasis on everyday opportunities

5. **Critical Thinking**
   - Include "What they're NOT" sections
   - Teach media literacy alongside inspiration
   - Acknowledge that role models are human

---

## Role Model Selection Criteria

### Must-Have Qualities

✅ **Documented positive character traits**
- Clear evidence of perseverance, kindness, courage, etc.
- Stories backed by reliable sources
- Consistent pattern of behavior, not one-off events

✅ **Overcame significant obstacles**
- Relatable challenges children might face
- Clear examples of resilience
- Teaching moments in their journey

✅ **Diverse representation**
- Various races, genders, nationalities
- Different fields (sports, science, arts, activism)
- Mix of contemporary and historical figures

✅ **Age-appropriate story elements**
- No graphic violence or trauma details
- Suitable content for children
- Positive messaging throughout

✅ **Public information available**
- Documented biography
- Reliable sources (books, interviews, documentaries)
- Verifiable achievements and character traits

### Exclusion Criteria

❌ **Controversial figures**
- Active legal issues or scandals
- Problematic past behavior
- Divisive political figures

❌ **Famous for appearance/wealth only**
- Reality TV stars without character examples
- Influencers without documented growth stories
- Lottery winners, inherited wealth

❌ **Insufficient documentation**
- Not enough verified information
- Primarily tabloid coverage
- Unclear character development journey

❌ **Recent scandals or allegations**
- Ongoing investigations
- Verified misconduct
- Behavioral inconsistencies with values we teach

## Story Format & Structure

### Story Template

```markdown
Title: [Role Model's Name] and the Power of [Character Trait]

Age Group: Kids (5-10) / Tweens (11-14) / Teens (15-17)

Duration: 2-4 minutes to read

---

INTRODUCTION (1-2 paragraphs)
- Who is this person?
- What are they known for?
- Why are they inspiring?

THE CHALLENGE (2-3 paragraphs)
- What obstacle did they face?
- How did it make them feel?
- Why was it difficult?

HOW THEY RESPONDED (2-3 paragraphs)
- Specific actions they took
- Character trait in action
- What they had to practice or learn
- Support they received

THE OUTCOME (1-2 paragraphs)
- What they learned (not just what they achieved)
- How the trait helped them
- How they continue to use this trait

UNIVERSAL LESSON (1 paragraph)
- How ANY child can apply this
- Everyday examples
- Process focus, not outcome

---

KEY LESSON (Highlighted):
One-sentence takeaway focusing on the character trait

DISCUSSION PROMPT (For Parents):
Question to ask your child about the story

MEDIA LITERACY NOTE:
"Remember: We're learning from [Name]'s character,
not trying to become them. Everyone's path is different!"
```

### Example Story

```markdown
Title: Simone Biles and the Power of Perseverance

Age Group: Kids (8-10)
Duration: 3 minutes

---

INTRODUCTION

Simone Biles is one of the greatest gymnasts in the world.
She's won more medals than almost anyone in history! But
what makes Simone truly special isn't just how many medals
she has—it's how she never gave up, even when things got
really hard.

THE CHALLENGE

When Simone was young, her life wasn't easy. She spent time
in foster care because her birth parents couldn't take care
of her. Later, her grandparents adopted her and her sister.
Simone started gymnastics when she was 6 years old during
a field trip.

As she got better, gymnastics became harder and harder. The
moves hurt. She fell many times. Other kids seemed naturally
talented, but Simone had to work extra hard on some skills.
Sometimes she wanted to quit.

HOW SHE RESPONDED

Instead of giving up, Simone decided to practice every single
day. She went to the gym for hours after school. When she fell,
she got back up. When a move scared her, she practiced it slowly
until she felt brave.

Simone also asked for help. Her coaches showed her new ways to
try difficult moves. Her family encouraged her to keep going.
But most importantly, Simone told herself: "I can learn this
if I keep trying."

Some days, she didn't improve at all. But she came back the
next day anyway. That's perseverance—trying again and again,
even when it's hard.

THE OUTCOME

Simone became an Olympic champion, but that's not the end of
the story. In 2021, during the Olympics, she decided to withdraw
from some events because she wasn't mentally ready. People called
this "quitting," but it was actually perseverance in a different
way—she persevered in taking care of herself.

Simone learned that perseverance doesn't mean pushing through
when you're hurt. It means keeping going in a smart way, asking
for help, and taking care of yourself so you can try again.

UNIVERSAL LESSON

You don't need to be an Olympic gymnast to practice perseverance.
Every time you:
- Try a hard math problem again after getting it wrong
- Practice a instrument even when your fingers hurt
- Keep playing a sport even after losing a game
- Learn to ride a bike after falling

...you're showing perseverance, just like Simone!

---

KEY LESSON:
"Perseverance isn't about being perfect. It's about trying
again after you make mistakes, and knowing when to ask for help."

DISCUSSION PROMPT:
"Tell me about a time you kept trying even when something
was hard. How did it feel when you didn't give up?"

MEDIA LITERACY NOTE:
Remember: Simone Biles is an amazing athlete, but we're not
trying to become Olympic gymnasts! We're learning about
perseverance—a trait that helps with homework, friendships,
learning new things, and life. Everyone can practice
perseverance in their own way.
```

## Activity Format & Structure

### Activity Template

```markdown
Activity Title: [Descriptive, Action-Oriented Title]

Character Trait: [Trait]
Age Range: [5-7 / 8-10 / 11-14 / 15-17]
Estimated Time: [10-15 minutes]
Difficulty: ⭐ to ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Materials Needed:
- [List any required items]
- [Most should need no special materials]

---

INTRODUCTION (Why we're doing this)
[1-2 sentences connecting to the character trait]

INSTRUCTIONS
1. [Clear, numbered steps]
2. [Age-appropriate language]
3. [Specific actions to take]
4. [Usually 4-6 steps]

WHAT TO NOTICE
[What should the child pay attention to?]
[How does this connect to the trait?]

REFLECTION QUESTIONS
1. [Question about the experience]
2. [Question about feelings]
3. [Question about future application]

PARENT TIP
[How parents can support or extend this activity]

---

MODIFICATIONS:
Easier: [Simpler version for younger or struggling children]
Harder: [More challenging version for older or advanced]
```

### Example Activity

```markdown
Activity Title: The 10-Minute Challenge

Character Trait: Perseverance
Age Range: 8-10
Estimated Time: 10 minutes
Difficulty: ⭐⭐

Materials Needed:
- Timer or phone
- Something challenging for your child (puzzle, drawing,
  sport skill, etc.)

---

INTRODUCTION
Perseverance means keeping going even when something is hard.
In this activity, you'll practice not giving up for 10 whole
minutes—just like Simone Biles practiced her gymnastics moves
over and over!

INSTRUCTIONS
1. Pick something you find challenging. It could be:
   - A puzzle you haven't finished
   - Drawing something detailed
   - Practicing a soccer trick
   - Building something with blocks
   - Learning a new song on an instrument

2. Set a timer for 10 minutes

3. Try your best for the full 10 minutes WITHOUT giving up

4. If you get frustrated, take 3 deep breaths and keep going

5. When the timer goes off, celebrate that you kept trying!

WHAT TO NOTICE
- Pay attention to when it feels hard. What do you feel in
  your body? What thoughts come to your mind?

- Notice what you tell yourself when it gets difficult.
  Are you being kind to yourself?

- See how you feel at the end—proud? Tired? Accomplished?

REFLECTION QUESTIONS
1. What was the hardest part? How did you keep going?

2. What did you tell yourself when you wanted to stop?

3. If a friend wanted to give up on something hard, what
   would you tell them now?

PARENT TIP
If your child gets very frustrated, remind them that
perseverance doesn't mean suffering! Help them take a breath,
try a different approach, or break the challenge into smaller
steps. The goal is to practice "trying again," not to force
perfectionism.

---

MODIFICATIONS:

Easier (Ages 5-7):
Reduce to 5 minutes and pick a very specific, simple task
like "try to button your coat 10 times" or "practice tying
your shoes."

Harder (Ages 11+):
Extend to 15-20 minutes and choose something that requires
sustained focus, like writing a short story, learning a
choreographed dance, or solving a complex puzzle.
```

- Background in education or child development
- Experience writing for children
- Strong research skills
- **Rate:** $30-50/hour or per-story rate

**Child Development Advisor (Consultant):**
- Review content for age-appropriateness
- Ensure psychological safety
- Provide guidance on trait development
- **Rate:** $75-150/hour, ~5 hours

**You (Founder):**
- Final content approval
- Strategic direction
- Quality control

## Content Maintenance

### Quarterly Updates
- Add 10-15 new role models
- Write 30-45 new stories
- Create 200 new activities
- Update existing content as needed

### Content Review Process
1. **Research:** Identify potential role models
2. **Vetting:** Check criteria (positive traits, no scandals)
3. **Writing:** Create stories following template
4. **Advisor Review:** Check age-appropriateness and safety
5. **Editing:** Refine language and structure
6. **Final Approval:** Founder sign-off
7. **Upload:** Add to database with proper tagging

### Handling Controversial Role Models

**If a role model becomes controversial:**
1. Assess severity (scandal vs. mistake)
2. Consider removing or adding context
3. Replace with alternative role model if needed
4. Notify users if content is removed
5. Learn from incident for future vetting

**Example Response:**
"We've decided to remove [Name] from our role model library
due to recent events. We're committed to featuring individuals
who consistently demonstrate positive character traits. We've
added [Alternative Name] who exemplifies [trait] in inspiring ways."

## Media Literacy Content

### 12 Core Lessons (Phase 2)

**For Kids (5-10):**

1. **Real vs. Edited**
   - Photos can be changed with computers
   - What you see isn't always how someone really looks
   - Activity: Compare edited vs. unedited photos

2. **Everyone Has Hard Days**
   - Social media shows highlights, not everyday life
   - Role models have bad days too
   - Activity: Create "highlight reel vs. reality" drawing

3. **Teamwork Behind Success**
   - Celebrities have coaches, teachers, helpers
   - Success takes a team
   - Activity: List all the helpers in your life

4. **Your Own Special Path**
   - Everyone is different and that's good!
   - Comparing yourself to others isn't fair
   - Activity: Draw your unique path to your goals

**For Tweens/Teens (11-17):**

1. **The Filter Effect**
   - Beauty filters distort reality
   - Natural bodies and faces are beautiful
   - Activity: Analyze before/after filter photos

2. **Behind the Celebrity**
   - Personal trainers, stylists, chefs, PR teams
   - Resources and support most don't have
   - Activity: Calculate cost of celebrity lifestyle

3. **Failure Stories**
   - Every role model has failed
   - Failure is part of growth
   - Activity: Research a role model's biggest failure

4. **Different Success Paths**
   - No single "right" way to develop a trait
   - Your timeline is your own
   - Activity: Create your own success roadmap

5. **Mental Health Reality**
   - Fame doesn't equal happiness
   - Money doesn't solve internal struggles
   - Activity: Reflect on what truly makes you happy

6. **Your Worth ≠ Your Achievements**
   - You're valuable just for being you
   - Character matters more than accomplishments
   - Activity: List non-achievement things you love about yourself

## Content Quality Standards

### Writing Guidelines

**Do:**
- Use age-appropriate vocabulary
- Write in active voice
- Be specific and concrete
- Include sensory details
- Focus on universal lessons
- Highlight process over outcome
- Show, don't tell

**Don't:**
- Use overly complex language
- Include graphic details
- Emphasize physical appearance
- Focus solely on achievements
- Make unrealistic comparisons
- Use shame or guilt
- Oversimplify complex issues

### Age-Appropriate Language

**Kids (5-10):**
- Short sentences (10-15 words)
- Common vocabulary
- Concrete examples
- Simple emotions (happy, sad, scared, brave)

**Tweens (11-14):**
- Moderate sentences (15-20 words)
- Introduce some complex vocabulary with context
- More nuanced emotions
- Abstract concepts explained concretely

**Teens (15-17):**
- Varied sentence length
- Advanced vocabulary
- Complex emotional landscapes
- Abstract thinking encouraged

## User-Generated Content (Phase 3)

### Local Hero Submissions

**Guidelines for Parents:**
- Must know the person personally
- Can verify the story
- Permission from the role model (if living)
- No promotional content
- Age-appropriate material

**Moderation Process:**
1. Automated screening (profanity, inappropriate content)
2. Human review (story quality, appropriateness)
3. Approval or feedback for revision
4. Publication (private to family or public)

**Quality Standards:**
- Follows story template structure
- Demonstrates clear character trait
- Age-appropriate content
- Well-written and engaging
- Verified information
